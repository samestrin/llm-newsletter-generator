From CUDA to MLX: How K-Search Brings Decades of Kernel Expertise to Apple Silicon

The Berkeley Artificial Intelligence Research Blog · 2026-07-29

Flagged: unchecked, this claim could not be checked

As AI hardware diversifies beyond NVIDIA's GPUs into Apple Silicon and custom accelerators, one challenge keeps surfacing: decades of hard-won CUDA kernel expertise—hand-tuned attention implementations, optimized state space model kernels, and countless engineering hours—don't automatically carry over to new architectures. Rather than rebuilding that knowledge from scratch, this work extends K-Search, an evolutionary kernel optimization framework from UC Berkeley Sky Lab, with a structured CUDA-to-MLX translation layer that transforms existing CUDA kernels into high-quality Apple Silicon code. The approach pairs an LLM acting as a "GPU kernel performance engineer" with a persistent decision-tree world model that scores, ranks, and refines optimization strategies—such as replacing threadgroup-memory reductions with register-only SIMD operations—while real hardware benchmarks ground the search in measurable results. The payoff speaks for itself: near-expert performance at 0.97x the speed of the native MLX Attention kernel, and up to a 20x prefill speedup over the community mlx-lm implementation on the Mamba SSM kernel—demonstrating that CUDA's accumulated wisdom can be translated, not just copied, across hardware ecosystems.
